# Invitation and briefing for analysts

## Invitation (send from the owner)

> **Subject: Help us test an AI analyst assistant: about 2 hours a week for 2–3 weeks**
>
> Hi <name>,
>
> We are trying out a platform that answers data questions, investigates why numbers moved, and drafts dashboards and
> reports when you describe what you need in plain words. Before we decide anything, we want to know whether it
> actually saves you time without getting things wrong. You are the best judge of that.
>
> What we ask: from <start date>, work through 12 short tasks over two weeks, each done twice: once your usual way and
> once with the platform. Time yourself, check the result as you normally would, and tell us honestly how it went.
> Results that go badly are as useful to us as good ones.
>
> The data is demo data (incident tickets, retail orders, process logs). Nothing you do is sent outside the platform,
> and nothing is published or sent to anyone without an approver saying yes.
>
> A 30-minute kick-off is on <date/time>. Reply if you can take part, or if the timing does not work.
>
> Thank you, <owner>

## Day-one briefing (30 minutes, coordinator)

**What this is.** A test of the platform, not of you. We compare your usual way with the platform on the same tasks,
and we keep every result: good, bad, or abandoned.

**What you get.**
* Your own login (never share it) and the analyst role in three demo workspaces.
* Your usual tools for the manual runs (a read-only data login or spreadsheet extracts of the same tables).
* The task cards, the timing sheet and the observation form.

**How to do a card.**
1. Read the card. The coordinator says whether you start with the manual run or the platform run.
2. Start the clock when you begin and stop it when the result is ready to hand over: checked and corrected.
   Pause the clock for breaks only.
3. In the platform, count every time you had to step in: rephrasing, answering a question it asked, correcting a number,
   re-running, or asking the workspace owner or the approver for something.
4. Fill a row of the timing sheet. Then in the app: turn on **Show expert tools** (bottom of the menu), then **Overview → Pilot scorecard → Record an
   assessment**, choose **Real analyst pilot observation**, choose what you assessed, and copy the row in.
5. Leave **Accuracy** at "Not reviewed yet": the reviewer sets it.

**Ground rules.**
* Check the platform's numbers the way you would check a colleague's. If something looks wrong, note it; do not quietly fix it in your head.
* Do not compare notes on a card with other analysts until everyone has finished it.
* Do not look at the answer key.
* If the platform blocks you, say so in the daily check-in. Do not work around it with someone else's login.
* It may say "I can't answer that from this data". That can be the right answer (card C12 tests exactly that).

**Where to get help.** Coordinator: <name>. Platform problems: technical owner <name>. Approvals: approver <name>.
